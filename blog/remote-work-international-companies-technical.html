How to Work Remotely for International Companies from South Africa

Last updated: 2026-07-03

By Brandon Katz. PaidProperly helps South Africans compare remote work platforms, payment routes, tax basics and practical earning tools. Learn more on the About page.

1. Tax & Legal

Understanding the South African Revenue Service (SARS) requirements is crucial for anyone working remotely for international companies. Here’s what you need to know:

  • Foreign Income: Ensure that you declare any foreign income. SARS requires individuals to disclose income earned outside South Africa.
  • Tax Clearance: Obtain a tax clearance certificate to prove that your tax affairs are in order.
  • Provisional Tax: If you’re earning substantial foreign income, registering as a provisional taxpayer might be necessary.

2. Payment Methods

Choosing the right payment method to receive international payments can save money and time. Compare these options:

  • Wise: Known for low fees and real exchange rates. Great for transferring money internationally with ease.
  • PayPal: Convenient and widely accepted but may have higher fees and currency conversion costs.
  • Skrill: An alternative to PayPal with some lower fees but limited integration options.
  • Cryptocurrency: Offers decentralization and lower costs but requires understanding of wallets and market volatility.
  • Bank Transfers: Direct but often slower and can incur significant fees especially for currency conversion.

3. Platforms

To start working internationally, consider joining freelance platforms that cater to different skill sets:

  • Upwork: Requires detailed profile setup, competitive interface. Fees vary from 10% to 20% of the project cost.
  • Fiverr: Gig-based platform. Charges 20% service fee on earnings.
  • Toptal: Premium platform, highly competitive, with specialized freelancer vetting process.
  • PeoplePerHour: Flexible work mechanics, with lower service fees than other platforms, around 5-20%.

4. Step-by-Step Action Plan

  1. Register on Platform: Choose a platform like Upwork and create a profile.
  2. Set up Payment: Create a Wise account to handle transaction settlements efficiently.
  3. Tax Registration: Register with SARS for your tax number if not available yet.
  4. Get your first Client: Enhance your profile with best practices and start bidding on projects.

5. Fee Calculator

To accurately track what you will take home after platform and payment fees, use a fee calculator specific to the platform you use. Understanding the difference between gross and net income will help avoid financial surprises.

6. Common Mistakes

Technical pitfalls can hinder smooth remote work. Here are some common issues:

  • Not understanding tax obligations leading to legal trouble.
  • Choosing the wrong payment method, causing unexpected losses.
  • Underestimating platform competition and necessary personal branding.
