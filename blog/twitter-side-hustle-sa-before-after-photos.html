This South African is Making Thousands on X Just Posting Google Street View Screenshots

Published February 11, 2026 • 8 min read

You don't need to code, teach English, or do data labelling to earn in dollars from South Africa. Sometimes all you need is Google Street View and a posting schedule.

A verified X (formerly Twitter) account called @jozivsjozi has built a massive following doing something remarkably simple: posting side-by-side screenshots of Johannesburg streets—one from around 2010, one from 2024.

No commentary. No video editing. No fancy tools. Just two images, a street address, and a posting schedule.

A recent post showing 114 Goud Street pulled 2.5 million views, 8,500 likes, and 1,200 reposts. Under X's creator revenue-sharing programme, those numbers translate directly into real income—paid in USD.

How Much Can You Actually Earn?

X pays verified creators a share of ad revenue generated from replies to their posts. The exact CPM (cost per thousand impressions) varies, but creators consistently report earning between R50–R90 per 1,000 impressions (roughly $3–$5 USD) for accounts with engaged audiences.

For South Africans looking for legitimate ways to earn online, this is one of the more creative approaches out there.

Why This Format Goes Viral in South Africa

The before-and-after format taps into something deeply emotional for South Africans. Urban decay in cities like Johannesburg, Durban, and Pretoria is a topic everyone has an opinion on. Every post becomes a comment section battleground—and that engagement is exactly what the X algorithm rewards.

Here's why it works so well:

  • It costs nothing. Google Street View is free. You don't need a camera, drone, or editing software. Your phone and an X Premium subscription (around R140/month) are your only expenses.
  • It requires no skill. Unlike freelancing on platforms where you need specific skills to get started, this side hustle has essentially zero barrier to entry beyond consistency.
  • It's infinitely repeatable. Joburg alone has thousands of streets with historical Street View imagery. You could post daily for years without running out of content.
  • The content is emotionally charged by default. You don't need to write a compelling caption. The images speak for themselves, and people can't help but react.
  • It's algorithm-friendly. Image comparison posts get high dwell time—people stop scrolling to study both photos. X's algorithm interprets that as high-quality content and pushes it to more feeds.

South African Cities You Could Cover

@jozivsjozi has locked down the Johannesburg niche. But South Africa has no shortage of cities with dramatic visual transformation. Here are untapped opportunities:

  • Durban (eThekwini) – The beachfront and CBD have changed enormously. Point Road, the old Addington area, and parts of the Berea are visually unrecognisable. Strong emotional connection for KZN residents.
  • Pretoria (Tshwane) – Church Street, Sunnyside, and the CBD offer stark contrasts. Pretoria also has a large, engaged online community.
  • Cape Town – A different angle here. Gentrification in Woodstock, Sea Point's transformation, and new developments could show positive change—or spark debate about who benefits from it.
  • Port Elizabeth (Gqeberha) – Often overlooked, but the CBD has deteriorated visibly. Locals would engage heavily.
  • Bloemfontein – Smaller audience but less competition. Could own this niche entirely.
  • East London (Buffalo City) – Similar to PE—a city many have watched change, with very little content being made about it.
  • Smaller towns – Think Klerksdorp, Welkom, Springs, or Kempton Park. Residents of these towns are starved for content about their areas and would share aggressively.

Step-by-Step: How to Start This Side Hustle

1. Pick your city

Choose one you know well or one with obvious visual change. Don't compete with @jozivsjozi on Joburg—find your own niche.

2. Research on Google Street View

Open Google Maps, drop into Street View, and use the time slider (the small clock icon) to compare historical imagery. Most SA cities have coverage going back to 2009–2011.

3. Screenshot both eras

Keep it clean. Same angle, same framing. No filters or text overlays. The raw comparison is more powerful.

4. Create your X account and brand it

Something simple and memorable. "[City] Then vs Now" or a play on the city name. Add a clear bio and profile image.

5. Subscribe to X Premium

This costs around R140/month and is required to qualify for revenue sharing. You'll also need 500+ followers and 5 million organic impressions in the last 3 months.

6. Post consistently

Daily is ideal. The algorithm rewards accounts that post regularly. Schedule posts for peak SA engagement times—typically 7–9am, 12–1pm, and 7–9pm.

7. Keep captions minimal

Just the street address and the years. Let the images do the talking. Don't editorialize—let the comments section debate for you.

8. Engage with replies

The more replies your posts get, the more ad revenue you generate. Respond to comments, ask questions, and encourage discussion.

The Catch: Getting to the Threshold

The biggest hurdle is reaching 5 million impressions in 3 months to qualify for X's revenue-sharing programme. That's roughly 55,000 impressions per day on average.

This is achievable but not easy. You'll likely need a few viral or semi-viral posts to get there.

Tips to accelerate growth:

  • Post your most dramatic before-and-after comparisons first to build momentum
  • Tag relevant accounts and use trending South African hashtags
  • Cross-post to other platforms (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook groups) to drive followers to your X account
  • Engage with other SA accounts in your niche

Realistically, expect 1–3 months of consistent posting before you hit the threshold and start earning.

How This Compares to Other Online Income

For South Africans exploring ways to earn money online from home, this side hustle sits in an interesting space. It requires almost no upfront skill or investment, but the income is unpredictable and depends entirely on going viral.

Compare that to more stable remote work options—freelancing, customer support, data labelling, or teaching—where the income is lower per hour but more consistent.

Most South Africans earning online use a mix of approaches: a stable remote work platform for baseline income, combined with higher-upside plays like content creation.
